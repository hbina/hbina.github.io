I have been reading and listening to many works by Ray Dalio, and I have learnt many things from him.
I don't hear people talk about radical transparency often, so I very much appreciate his discussion of it.
I agree with the principle in general. However, I would like to propose an improvement, mostly around the framing.

1. Being transparent does not give you agency. Transparency means anyone can see everything inside, and you cannot do anything about it.
2. To prevent this, you then have to be selectively transparent, and here you are being arbitrary. How selective? What should and shouldn't be disclosed? To whom?

Consider this paragraph:

> Don’t get me wrong: Radical transparency isn’t the same as total transparency. It just means much more transparency than is typical. We do keep some things confidential, such as private health matters or deeply personal problems, sensitive details about intellectual property or security issues, the timing of a major trade, and at least for the short term, matters that are likely to be distorted, sensationalized, and harmfully misunderstood if leaked to the press.

I would like to propose a better framing: radical honesty.
With honesty, there is no "choice" to be made.
If someone asks you about personal issues and you don't want to disclose that information, the honest response is exactly that: you don't want to disclose that information.
Honesty is also a much more powerful principle because it applies to your *intrapersonal* relationship—your relationship with yourself, with whom you are already fully transparent.
